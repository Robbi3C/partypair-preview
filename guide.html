The Cape Cod Bachelorette Guide

Everything we've learned styling bachelorette weekends across the Cape — the towns, the timing, the itinerary that never fails.

First: pick your town (it decides everything)

Cape Cod isn't one destination — it's fifteen small ones strung along seventy miles of coast, and each has a completely different energy. Pick the town first and the rest of the weekend plans itself.

Falmouth & Woods Hole — the easy win

Closest to the bridges, so the drive from Boston is shortest and late arrivals on Friday aren't a crisis. Walkable Main Street, good restaurants, and the ferry to Martha's Vineyard leaves from here if you want an island day. Best for: groups with mixed arrival times and anyone planning a Vineyard day trip.

Hyannis & Yarmouth — the nightlife pick

The closest thing the Cape has to a downtown scene: bars, live music, and plenty of big rental houses nearby. Ferries to Nantucket run from Hyannis. Best for: groups who want to actually go out out.

Dennis & Harwich — the beach-house classic

Warm-water Nantucket Sound beaches, big decks, and that mid-Cape location where nothing is more than thirty minutes away. Best for: groups splitting time between beach days and day trips.

Chatham — the polished one

The preppy, postcard-perfect elbow of the Cape. Boutique shopping, oysters, seal cruises, and the most photogenic Main Street in Massachusetts. Best for: groups whose vibe is "matching linen sets and a raw bar."

Provincetown — the party at the end of the world

Drag brunches, dancing until late, art galleries, whale watches, and the best people-watching on the eastern seaboard. Best for: groups who want maximum fun-per-hour and don't plan on sleeping much.

💡 Local tip: book the house before anything else. The best group-sized rentals on the Cape are reserved 6–10 months out for summer weekends — earlier than restaurants, boats, or anything else you'll book.

The 3-day itinerary that never fails

Friday — arrival night

  • Stagger arrivals; assign someone (not the MOH) as grocery captain.
  • The walk-in moment: if the house is decorated before anyone arrives, Friday night starts at a ten. This is, not coincidentally, what we do.
  • Dinner in — a big pasta night or local takeout tastes better than a rushed reservation everyone's late for.

Saturday — the main event

  • Morning: slow start, beach or pool, matching-outfit photos while everyone's fresh.
  • Afternoon: the one booked activity — a private sail, oyster-shucking class, winery visit, or seal cruise. One is enough; two is a forced march.
  • Evening: the nice dinner. Book restaurants for groups of 8+ at least a month ahead — Cape restaurants are small and summer is merciless.

Sunday — the soft landing

  • Bagels, beach walk, pack-up. Keep it unstructured.
  • If checkout cleanup is looming over the weekend, outsource it — your future selves will pay almost anything on Sunday morning.

When to book what

  • 6–10 months out: the rental house.
  • 3–4 months out: decor/setup service (we take one party per weekend — summer sells out by spring), boats and private activities.
  • 1–2 months out: Saturday dinner reservation, custom merch orders.
  • 2 weeks out: grocery list, itinerary share, Venmo requests before the trip, not after.

Budget without the group-chat drama

The math that keeps friendships intact: total the fixed costs (house, decor, activity, bride's share split among the group), divide once, and collect before the weekend. Then everyone covers their own travel, and you pool a flat amount per person for groceries and drinks. No receipts, no spreadsheets on vacation, no one Venmo-requesting $4.50 for limes in August.
